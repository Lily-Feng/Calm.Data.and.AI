The constraint the paper removed

Recurrent sequence models compute position t from position t−1. That dependency is a hard serialisation: no matter how many accelerators you own, a single sequence is processed one step at a time, and information from the start of the sequence reaches the end only by passing through every intermediate state.

Attention had already been bolted onto recurrent models as a shortcut. The paper's claim is that once you have the shortcut, you can throw away the road.

Three consequences

  • Constant path length. Any two positions are one attention step apart, so long-range dependencies do not degrade with distance.
  • Parallel training. All positions are computed at once, which turns a sequence model into a matrix-multiplication workload — exactly what GPUs are for.
  • Quadratic cost. Every position attends to every other, so compute grows with the square of sequence length. Most architecture work since has been about this line item.

Why it mattered more than it looked

Read in 2017, this is a machine-translation paper with a competitive BLEU score. Its significance is architectural: it made model quality a function of compute spent rather than of research cleverness. Once scaling laws made that relationship explicit, capability became a budgeting exercise, and the field's constraint moved from ideas to data centres.

What to carry away

  • Removing a serial dependency can matter more than improving the metric it was serialising for.
  • An architecture that maps cleanly onto the hardware you can buy will outrun a better one that does not.
  • Read the cost table. The quadratic term is the whole subsequent research programme.
